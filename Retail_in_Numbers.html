2023

Induced jobs are an estimate (see ‘i’). Based on ONS input-output tables and workforce jobs by industry; the input-output tables lag other data by around two years.
Historical average (2017-2023)2.77mn
Jobs supported elsewhere in the economy (supply chain + induced), household-closed IO model on a jobs basis, SIC 46-47
These are jobs the retail and wholesale industry supports elsewhere in the economy, on top of the people who work in shops and warehouses themselves (they're counted on the Jobs card). Two things drive it. The first is the supply chain: jobs at the firms the industry buys from, like the farms, factories, hauliers and IT suppliers that keep shops stocked and running, plus the firms that supply them in turn. The second, which we label induced, is knock-on spending. The industry and its suppliers pay wages, and when those workers spend their money on things like rent, the weekly shop or a night out, that keeps other people in work too. ‘All jobs’ adds the two together. To work it out we use an input-output model, the standard approach for this kind of question, based on ONS tables that show how every part of the economy buys from and pays every other part. We count it in jobs, using ONS workforce jobs by industry (the same JOBS03 and JOBS04 figures behind our retail jobs number), and we take out any jobs that sit within retail itself so nothing is counted twice. The supply-chain figure is a firm count. The induced figure is more of an estimate, because it depends on how much of people's pay gets spent back into the UK economy, so treat it as indicative. The ONS tables come out with a delay, so this card sits around two years behind the rest.

Rateable value of retail properties, GB
The property tax bill retail faces. Every shop is given a “rateable value” – an estimate of what it would rent for in a year – and the tax owed is worked out from that.
